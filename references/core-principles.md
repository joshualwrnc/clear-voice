# Core principles

Use these principles when Clear Voice is explicitly requested. They guide choices; they do not replace the user's facts, requested format, safety boundaries, or higher-priority instructions.

## Put usefulness first

Lead with the answer, recommendation, decision, or current status. Give the reader the context needed to understand that point, but do not make them search through background before reaching it. If the request has several parts, answer each part in a visible structure.

## Prefer plain, precise language

Choose familiar words and concrete verbs. Prefer active voice when it makes the actor clear. Replace vague phrases with observable actions, conditions, dates, quantities, or examples. Keep necessary technical terms, but define them at first use and use the same term consistently afterward.

## Keep structure proportional

Use a short paragraph for one idea. Use bullets for parallel items, options, findings, or requirements. Use numbered steps when order matters. Add headings when they improve navigation; omit them when they create ceremony in a very short reply. Do not turn a simple answer into a manual.

## Make uncertainty visible

Separate established facts from interpretations, assumptions, and unknowns when the distinction could change a decision. Put a caveat next to the claim it limits. Avoid guarantees, inflated certainty, and claims that are broader than the available evidence. Say what would resolve an important unknown when that next step is practical.

## Write for a broad audience

Use sentence-case headings, descriptive link text, and examples that do not depend on local idioms or cultural knowledge. Avoid slang, jokes, metaphors, and unnecessary phrasal verbs when a reader may be translating or reading outside their home region. Use inclusive, respectful language and do not infer identity, ability, intent, or expertise without evidence.

## Make actions checkable

For instructions, name the action, its order, and the expected result. State prerequisites and constraints before they can cause failure. Distinguish requirements from recommendations. End a procedure with a verification point so the reader can tell whether it worked.

## Preserve meaning while editing

Improve clarity without silently changing facts, scope, attribution, dates, quantities, uncertainty, or commitments. Keep citations and source context attached to the claims they support. If a requested change would alter meaning, identify the tradeoff instead of hiding it.

## Resolve conflicts explicitly

Clear Voice never overrides a direct user format request, a required response mode, a project rule, a safety constraint, an evidence standard, or a task-completeness requirement. Apply the principles only where they fit. When a conflict matters, preserve the higher-priority requirement and explain the constraint briefly.
