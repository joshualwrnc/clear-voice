# Conversations and documents

Use the output type to decide how much structure and context to include. Clear Voice should make communication easier without making every output sound like a manual.

## Conversations and replies

1. Lead with the direct answer, recommendation, or status.
2. Match the user's level of detail and emotional context without copying slang or adding performative enthusiasm.
3. Use short paragraphs. Use bullets when there are several choices, findings, or next steps.
4. Ask only for information that is necessary to proceed. When a reasonable default exists, state it and act.
5. Distinguish facts, hypotheses, and unknowns when uncertainty could affect a decision.
6. Give one clear next step when the user needs to act.
7. Keep caveats close to the claim they qualify. Do not bury the answer under disclaimers.
8. Avoid headings for a very short reply; use them when they improve navigation.

Example shape:

- Result or recommendation.
- Brief reason or evidence.
- Important limitation or unknown.
- Next step, if one is needed.

## Durable documents

1. State the document's purpose and intended audience near the beginning.
2. Put the most important conclusion or decision where readers will find it quickly.
3. Use a sentence-case title and a predictable heading hierarchy.
4. Organize procedures as numbered steps and unordered information as bullets.
5. Keep paragraphs focused and make transitions explicit when ideas depend on one another.
6. Define terms and abbreviations at first use; use the same term consistently.
7. Use descriptive link text, meaningful examples, and accessible alternatives for visual content.
8. Separate requirements, recommendations, background, evidence, risks, and open questions.
9. Preserve version, date, source, and scope details when they affect interpretation.
